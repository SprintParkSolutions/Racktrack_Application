Where we are today

The product now runs as a coherent, multi-tenant platform. A datacenter technician photographs a rack and, in a few seconds, sees every device and port identified — with cable type, colour and connection status — all organized under a clean Owner → Organization → Site → Members structure so each team only ever sees its own work. This session pushed on all fronts at once: the interface was unified into a single confident visual language, the organization and permissions model was completed and made real, the detection pipeline got noticeably smarter and more honest about its own confidence, the live switch integration went from blocked to reading real port data, and the codebase took a meaningful step toward production.

02

What we built

Five parallel workstreams, each moved forward this session.

Design & experience

Shipped

The entire app was brought onto one visual language — a clean, white-focused surface with confident black accents — so every screen feels like the same product. Screens that had drifted into dark, hard-to-read states were rebuilt, and the layout was made to fit every device from a small phone to a desktop.

  • Unified every screen to white surfaces with subtle-black accents; removed the inconsistent dark/greyed states.
  • Made the app fit any screen — phones, iPad, and desktop — with no clipped content or sideways scroll.
  • Redesigned the organization dashboard to be minimal, scannable, and responsive.
  • Polished the details techs actually touch: password reveal, one-tap code entry, readable labels, a clean in-app switch console, and a proper back button on the located-port screen.

Organizations & access control

Shipped

The multi-tenant backbone is now real and usable. There's a clear hierarchy, a role for every kind of user, dashboards for oversight, and full lifecycle control over members — including inviting them by link so they set their own password.

  • A working Owner & Org-Admin console with dashboards: organizations, sites, users, and scan activity at a glance.
  • Role-aware sign-in — the form adapts to who's logging in (member, admin, owner) while permissions always come from the account, never the choice.
  • Full member management: add, edit, deactivate/reactivate, and invite by link (the invitee opens the link and picks their own username & password).
  • Access is correctly scoped — owners see everything, admins see their whole organization, members see their own site — across scans, results, images and ports.

Detection intelligence

Shipped

The computer-vision pipeline got both smarter and more trustworthy. Patch-panel counting now matches real hardware, only the devices that actually have ports can be selected, colours are shown truthfully, and the model tells the technician when it isn't sure.

  • Patch panels now handle 12, 24 and 48 ports — the count is reconciled to the real panel size instead of a fixed guess.
  • Device selection is limited to port-bearing gear (switch, patch panel, router, gateway, firewall) — no more selecting a server with no ports.
  • Cable colour is shown truthfully again (the swatch matches the detected colour), and a low-confidence prompt asks the tech to verify when the photo resolution is poor.
  • A visual overview shows the rack with every detected device and port drawn on it.

Live switch integration

Shipped

The "Ports & Drift" capability — reading a switch's live port state and comparing it to what was scanned — went from blocked to working. RackTrack now connects to the switch, runs read-only commands, and returns real port data.

  • RackTrack connects to the live switch, runs read-only queries, and returns real port status.
  • Works with the switch's older security profile, so no hardware change was required to get connected.
  • Lays the groundwork for physical-drift detection — flagging when what's plugged in differs from the record.

Production readiness

Shipped

The codebase took a real step toward being deployable and safe — hardening the server, cleaning the build, and adding the packaging and quality tooling a production service needs.

  • Hardened the server: security headers, sign-in rate-limiting, and closing an unauthenticated remote-access hole.
  • Made the shipped app actually reach its backend, and restored automatic sign-out on expired sessions.
  • Added containerization, CI, and code-quality tooling, and removed a large amount of dead code.
  • Produced a clear hardening checklist of the remaining operational steps.
03

How it flows

The core journeys, end to end — useful for walking through the demo.

Onboarding & organization lifecycle
1
The owner sets up organizations
The platform owner creates an organization directly, or a company self-registers as a request.
2
Self-registrations are approved
A company that signs itself up starts as a pending request that the owner reviews and approves before it becomes active.
3
The org admin builds out the team
Once active, the organization's admin adds sites and brings in members — two ways: send an invite link (they set their own password) or add them manually.
4
Members scan; everyone sees their slice
Members scan racks at their site. Visibility rolls up automatically — member → site, admin → organization, owner → everything.
Scan to insight — the technician's journey
1
Capture
The tech photographs the rack from their phone; tall racks are stitched from several frames.
2
Analyze
In a few seconds the app returns the rack with every device and port identified and drawn on the image.
3
Select a device
The tech picks a port-bearing device — a switch, patch panel, router, gateway or firewall.
4
Locate a port
They enter a port number (and can switch port type in place); the exact port is highlighted on the device.
5
Read the result
Connection status, cable type and colour — with a confidence read, and a nudge to double-check when the model isn't sure.
6
Confirm & report
One-tap feedback improves the model for the whole organization; results export to a report and can raise a ticket or cross-check live network truth.
The detection pipeline — under the hood
1
Capture & stitch
Normalize the photo; combine multi-frame captures of tall racks into one aligned image.
2
Detect devices
Recognize every device and place it at its position in the rack.
3
Detect ports & status
Identify each port and whether it's connected or empty; reconstruct patch panels to their true 12/24/48-port grid.
4
Classify cables
Read the cable type and colour on every connected port.
5
Read labels
Lift printed device labels and nameplates for identification.
6
Assemble
Produce the annotated image plus a structured record, claimed to the technician's site.
Live switch & drift
1
Connect
RackTrack reaches the switch securely and signs in with a read-only account.
2
Query
It runs read-only commands to see which ports are live and what's attached.
3
Compare
Live truth is set against the scanned record to surface physical drift — the basis for keeping documentation honest over time.
