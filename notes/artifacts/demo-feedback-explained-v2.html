F1

Switch login & live detail — surfaced

“Confirm and fully expose switch login functionality for all supported devices directly from RackTrack.”

Surfaced today

What we did today

Built the “Live Network Switch” (Ports) page: it logs into the switch and shows the full live read — identity (model, firmware, uptime), the port faceplate, PoE draw, VLANs, and neighbours — all over SSH. The capability existed on the backend; now it's visible on screen for a real device.

What's left

Prove it across the full list of supported models — which needs real switch hardware in hand. We can only show one today because that's the one switch we can reach.

What to tell

“Switch login is live and on screen — you're seeing a real switch read end to end. Covering every vendor model is a testing pass once we have the devices.”

F2

Traceroute / ping — built today

“Add quick verification/troubleshooting actions (for example, a ‘Traceroute’ button) accessible from the rack view.”

Built today

What we did today

Added a Reachability check to the switch page: type an IP or hostname and hit Ping or Traceroute, and it runs from the switch itself over the SSH channel we already have, showing the live result and hops. It rode the console plumbing exactly as expected — no new infrastructure.

Live output (from the switch)

TL-SG2428P# tracert 8.8.8.8 30
Tracing route to 8.8.8.8 over a maximum of 30 hops
 1   1 ms  192.168.1.1  ← your gateway
 2   1 ms  183.82.0.1  ← ISP
 3  *  *  *  Request timed out.
 4  10 ms  183.82.14.78
 …  (Google's routers)
 8  10 ms  8.8.8.8  ← reached
Trace complete.

What's left

The commands are vendor-aware (TP-Link uses tracert, Cisco/D-Link use traceroute); the Cisco form is wired and will be confirmed once we have that hardware to test on.

What to tell

“You can ping or traceroute any target straight from the switch view and watch the hops live — the quick verification action, done.”

F7

Cable endpoints, Cables view & trace — built and shown

“Review how cable endpoints and path information are presented to ensure existing capabilities are clearly demonstrated.”

Built today

What we did today

Three things: the endpoint card on a located port (the device on the other end, by name or MAC, with VLAN); the Cables view that lists every live link; and the expandable end-to-end trace — a hop chain from the switch, through the port, to the endpoint, with the uplink expanding to every device behind it. All live from the switch.

What's left

This is the full logical trace. A complete physical trace needs two things one switch can't give: reachability to the neighbouring switches (to walk hop to hop) and patch-panel port labels (to fill the passive panels the network can't see).

What to tell

“Every port shows what's on the other end, and you can expand any cable into its full path. The logical trace is done; extending it through patch panels and across switches needs those switches reachable and the panels labelled.”
