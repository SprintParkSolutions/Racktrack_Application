The cloud decision

Which cloud,
and why?

Two genuinely strong platforms. I've gone through them area by area, set out what each is best at, and formed a view on which fits RackTrack. That view is mine as an engineer, and the call is yours.

First, why move at all

One server is
the weak point.

Everything today depends on a single machine we update by hand. Fine for a demo. A real risk once customers depend on us.

My read

The move matters more than the brand. Either cloud fixes all four problems on the right. That's why I'd rather we decide quickly than debate it for weeks.

1No backupsone dead disk erases every customer scan, permanently
2One point of failureif that server stops, the whole product stops
3Can't handle a rushno way to scale when many technicians scan at once
4Updated by handno test copy, no undo if something breaks

The two contenders

Both are
excellent.

I want to be fair here. This isn't a case where one is good and one is bad. They're both mature, both used by enormous companies, and either would serve us well.

The question is only which fits our product better.

AWS
Amazon's cloud. The largest and longest established, with the widest range of services and the biggest community.
Strength: the deepest AI and GPU toolkit
Azure
Microsoft's cloud. Equally capable, particularly strong inside organisations already built on Microsoft tools.
Strength: unmatched inside Microsoft estates

Area 1 of 6

Running
the app.

Both run our app in containers that copy themselves when busy and restart themselves if one crashes.

Both are strong here

AWS is the more battle tested, Azure is the quicker to stand up. Both end the "one server and we're down" problem, which is what we actually need.

Why it doesn't decide it: genuinely nothing to choose between them, so I wouldn't pick a cloud on this page.

AWS
Fargate. The most proven option, with the largest community and the most examples to follow. Slightly more setup the first time.
Strength: the most proven at scale
Azure
Container Apps. Newer and a little simpler to start, and it can idle down to nothing when unused.
Strength: simpler to get running

Area 2 of 6

The data
and backups.

This is the problem we most need solved. Both give us a proper managed PostgreSQL database with automatic backups, plus durable storage for the scan photos.

Both are strong here, and that's good news

AWS storage is the most widely used on the internet; Azure's managed database tooling is excellent. The most important fix we need is solved equally well either way.

Why it doesn't decide it: whichever we choose, we stop being one disk failure away from losing everything.

AWS
RDS PostgreSQL for the database, with automatic failover, plus S3 for the scan photos.
Strength: the most widely used storage anywhere
Azure
Azure Database for PostgreSQL plus Blob Storage. Feature for feature, the same capability.
Strength: excellent managed database tooling

Area 3 of 6 - the decider

The AI
scanning.

This is the one area where they genuinely differ, and it happens to be our heaviest and most expensive workload. The AI needs GPUs, which are specialised and costly.

Both are strong, but this is where they part

Azure has a capable AI platform and is superb if you build on Microsoft AI services. AWS has the wider choice of GPUs, better pricing on them, and the more mature AI tooling.

Why AWS is my choice: we don't use Microsoft AI services, so Azure's strength here isn't one we'd draw on. Meanwhile the biggest line on our future bill is GPUs, and that is exactly where AWS is strongest. Our scanning is bursty too, so GPUs can wake for a scan and shut down when idle, and we pay only while scanning.

AWS
Widest selection of GPU types, generally the best prices, and SageMaker, the most mature set of AI tools of any cloud.
Strength: widest GPU range, best GPU pricing
Azure
Fully capable through Azure ML, with a somewhat narrower GPU menu. It would work, it just isn't the strong point.
Strength: strong AI platform, superb if you use Microsoft AI services

Area 4 of 6

How people
sign in.

This is usually the reason teams choose Azure, because it plugs straight into Microsoft accounts. I looked at it carefully, and I don't think it applies to us.

Azure is genuinely better here, but we can't use it

Credit where it's due: Microsoft Entra is the best identity system of the two. If our users signed in with Microsoft accounts, that alone might decide this.

Why it doesn't decide it for us: our users are datacenter technicians signing in with accounts their own employer issues. They aren't Microsoft users, so we'd never draw on that strength. Both clouds handle the standard company sign in we actually need.

AWS
Cognito handles standard company sign-in, connecting to whatever identity system the customer already uses.
Strength: flexible, connects to any identity system
Azure
Microsoft Entra. Genuinely best in class if your users are on Microsoft accounts. Ours aren't, so we simply don't get to use that strength.
Strength: the best in the business for Microsoft logins

Area 5 of 6

Watching it,
and shipping safely.

Both give us live dashboards, alerts to our phones, and a safe one button way to release updates with an undo.

Azure is slightly nicer here

Being honest: Azure's dashboards are friendlier out of the box. AWS gives deeper control once you know it, but Azure is the easier start.

Why it doesn't change my choice: this is a comfort difference, not a capability one. It isn't enough to outweigh the GPU gap on our heaviest workload.

AWS
CloudWatch for dashboards and alerts, CodePipeline for automatic releases with rollback. Deep and mature.
Strength: deepest control once you know it
Azure
Azure Monitor with friendly dashboards, and deploy slots for instant swap and rollback.
Strength: friendliest dashboards out of the box

costs.

Broadly the same on both. The GPU choice drives the bill far more than the provider does.

Level pegging on price

Why AWS still edges it: not the sticker price, but the GPUs. Cheaper GPU rates plus scaling them to zero means our largest variable cost is lower over time.

Where AWS leads

AWS's real
strengths.

Before I pick a side, here is what each platform is genuinely best at. These are AWS's, and the first one lands directly on our heaviest workload.

The one that matters most to us

AI and GPU work. The scanning is the most expensive thing we do. AWS has the widest GPU choice, the keenest pricing, and the deepest machine learning toolkit of any cloud.

1The deepest AI and GPU toolkitwidest GPU range, best GPU pricing, and SageMaker, the most mature ML platform availableAWS
2The largest service catalogueif we ever need something unusual, AWS almost certainly already has a managed version of itAWS
3Biggest talent pool and documentationeasier to hire for, and far more answers online when we get stuckAWS
4The longest track record at scalethe most battle tested of the two, running some of the largest systems in the worldAWS
5The widest third party ecosystemmost tools and vendors integrate with AWS firstAWS

Where Azure leads

Azure's real
strengths.

It would be dishonest to reduce Azure to "good for Microsoft logins". These are the areas where it genuinely leads, and one of them deserves a proper conversation.

The one I want to flag

Hybrid and on-premises. Our customers are datacenter operators. Some may not want our software in a public cloud at all, and would rather run it on their own hardware. Azure is clearly the strongest platform for that.

1Running inside a customer's own datacenterAzure Arc and Azure Stack are the best in the industry for this. AWS has an answer, but it isn't as strongAzure
2Compliance and regulated industriesan exceptionally broad set of certifications, plus dedicated government regions. Matters if we sell to banks, healthcare or governmentAzure
3More geographic regionsrelevant to "where is our data allowed to live", which enterprise buyers always askAzure
4Can idle down to zero nativelyContainer Apps does this out of the box; the AWS equivalent needs more setupAzure
5Commercially easy for Microsoft customersif a buyer already has a Microsoft agreement, adding Azure is frictionlessAzure

An honest open question

One thing could
change my answer.

Everything I've recommended assumes we run RackTrack as a service that customers connect to. If instead we need to ship it into customers' own datacenters, Azure becomes the stronger choice and I'd change my recommendation.

I don't have visibility on that. It's a product and sales question, not an engineering one.

What I need to know

Do we expect customers to ask for an on-premises version? If the answer is yes, or even likely, tell me now and I'll re-run this comparison with that as a requirement.

Being fair about it

We do have
other options.

I focused on AWS and Azure because they're the two most likely to satisfy an enterprise buyer's questions. But they aren't the only paths, and I'd rather put the alternatives on the table than pretend it's a two horse race.

My read

I'd still choose a major cloud, because enterprise buyers ask who hosts us and recognise these names. But if cost or simplicity matters more than that, options 2 and 3 are legitimate.

1Google Cloudalso strong for AI work. I left it out mainly because it's less commonly asked for by enterprise buyers, not because it's weaker
2Stay where we are, add backupscheapest and fastest. Fixes the data risk but not the single server problem or scaling
3A simpler managed platformeasier and cheaper to run, but less of the enterprise paperwork buyers expect
4A hybridkeep the app where it is, move only storage and backups to a cloud. A halfway step

Putting it together

Two strong platforms.
Different strengths.

Neither is the better cloud. They lead in different places, and several categories are genuinely tied. So this isn't a scoreboard, it's a question of fit.

How I'd decide it

Ask which strengths we would actually use. Both lists below are real. The difference is that AWS's lead sits on the workload that will dominate our bill, while most of Azure's lead sits on things our product doesn't currently need.

My recommendation

I'd go AWS.

We're an AI product first. The scanning is the heaviest and most expensive thing we do, and it's the one area where the two genuinely differ. Choosing the platform that's strongest at our largest cost is the decision I can most easily defend.

Azure is an equally strong platform and leads in real areas. But those areas, hybrid deployment and Microsoft identity, are ones our product doesn't currently draw on. If that changes, so does my answer.

Three things that would change my mind

1. If customers will want RackTrack on their own hardware, Azure is stronger.
2. If we're targeting regulated buyers where compliance breadth matters.
3. If our own team is already fluent in Microsoft tooling, because familiarity beats any feature comparison.

Whichever we pick, I'd build on standard components so most of it could move later. I don't want this to be a one way door.

Over to you

The call is yours.

There will be things I can't see from where I sit. Existing contracts or credits. Cost constraints. A commercial relationship. Where customer data is allowed to live. Any of those could reasonably outweigh what's on these slides, and I'd want to know before we commit.

A direction, so I can start the first stage. If it's AWS I'll begin the foundation work. If you'd rather go another way, tell me why and I'll plan around it.
