RackTrack Site Audit

Every page on racktrack.ai, read and tested for security, speed, bugs, accessibility, typography, colour, layout, mobile behaviour, search visibility and the words themselves. Written in plain English so anyone on the team can act on it.

24 pages crawled143 assets checked48 page views tested (desktop + mobile)~18,500 words of copy read

Summary

The site looks good and the story it tells is clear. The problems are mostly under the surface: how the site is served, how heavy it is, and a handful of decisions in the code that work against visitors. None of them are hard to fix, and most are a day or two of work each. The counts below are grouped findings, not individual occurrences (a contrast problem that repeats on every page counts once, and the "smaller items" groups each hold several points).

8Critical
21High
23Medium
6Low groups

Critical = loses leads, exposes the company, or breaks the site for a group of people. High = clearly hurts trust, speed or usability. Medium = worth fixing in the next pass. Low = polish.

What is working well

Worth saying first, because a lot of care has gone in:

  • The visual identity is consistent across the marketing pages: one blue accent, one navy, one type family, a monospace used deliberately for labels and figures. It reads as one product.
  • The homepage scroll story on desktop is well built. It respects prefers-reduced-motion, the poster frames are already in the markup, and the phone-to-rack animation explains the product faster than text would.
  • Good accessibility foundations: a "Skip to content" link, proper <main>, <nav> and <footer> landmarks, visible keyboard focus in the nav, dropdowns that open on keyboard focus, and Escape closes the video modal.
  • Every page has a unique title and a meta description, headings are in order (one H1 per page), and lang="en" is set everywhere except the Knowledgebase.
  • Email is well protected: SPF is set with -all and DMARC is on with p=quarantine. That is better than most sites this size.
  • External links use rel="noopener"; no broken internal links or missing assets were found; no mixed-content warnings.
  • The Trust and Security page is honest about SOC 2 being "In progress" rather than implying certification.
  • The contact form validates inline with aria-invalid and a live status region, which is more than most mailto forms do.

Fix these first

If only ten things get done, these are the ten. Each is explained in detail further down.

  1. Force HTTPS and add security headers. http://racktrack.ai currently loads over plain HTTP with no redirect, and there is no HSTS, Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy or Permissions-Policy on any page. One .htaccess block fixes all of it.
  2. Replace the mailto contact form with a real submission. Today the "Request Platform Brief" button just opens the visitor's email app. Anyone on a work laptop without a mail client, or on a phone with webmail, gets nothing. Leads are being lost silently.
  3. Remove the "refresh sends you home" script. js/refresh-home.js runs on every page and redirects a browser reload on any page back to the homepage. Someone refreshing the pricing FAQ or an article lands on the home page and loses their place.
  4. Add a privacy policy, cookie policy and terms, and honour the cookie choice. The banner says "we use cookies for analytics", "Cookie settings" points to a page with no cookie text, and the GoDaddy tracking script loads whether the visitor accepts or declines. The form collects name, email and phone with no privacy notice.
  5. Cut the page weight. The homepage video is 59 MB; a full desktop scroll of the homepage downloads about 610 animation frames (roughly 31 MB); About Us and Why RackTrack are 9.5 MB each on a phone; the Knowledgebase is a single 8.4 MB HTML file. Resize, compress, add srcset, and stream the video.
  6. Move the cookie banner out of the way. It sits fixed over the navigation on desktop and over the page heading on mobile, on every page, until dismissed.
  7. Fix the contact form validation. It rejects "José", any name with an accent or non-Latin letters, and any phone number that is not exactly 10 digits, so UK, German and Australian numbers fail even though those country codes are offered.
  8. Give the Knowledgebase a viewport tag, lang, alt text, and a way back. On phones it renders as a zoomed-out desktop page. It also has literal "####" Markdown headings on screen and passages written for RackTrack staff rather than customers.
  9. Source or soften the numbers. "40-60% truth decay", "Why every enterprise CMDB is 40% wrong" (the article never gives the figure), "99.2% Truth fidelity" and "34% Manual operations reduced" have no source. Without JavaScript the About page literally reads "0% Truth fidelity".
  10. Pick one call to action. There are nine different labels for the same request (Get a demo, Request platform brief, Request Platform Brief, Request a platform brief, Request a Security Brief, Request a demo, See it in action, Contact RackTrack, Engage the platform team). Visitors cannot tell whether a "platform brief" is a PDF, a call or a demo.

Security and privacy

CriticalHTTP is not redirected to HTTPS

Seenhttp://racktrack.ai/ and http://www.racktrack.ai/ both return the full page with status 200 over plain HTTP. No redirect, no HSTS header.

Why it mattersAnyone who types the address or follows an old link gets an unencrypted page that can be altered in transit (for example on hotel or airport Wi-Fi). Search engines also treat the HTTP and HTTPS versions as separate sites.

We couldAdd a 301 redirect from HTTP to HTTPS in .htaccess, then send Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains. Once it has been stable for a while, consider HSTS preload.

CriticalNo security headers on any page

SeenResponses carry only server: Apache, cache-control and content-type. Missing: Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options (or CSP frame-ancestors), X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy. The same is true of portal.racktrack.ai (served by Caddy), which is the login page linked from "Sign In".

Why it mattersWithout frame protection the site (and the portal login) can be embedded in someone else's page for clickjacking. Without a CSP, any injected script runs freely. Security-minded buyers run exactly these checks (Mozilla Observatory, securityheaders.com) before a procurement call, and the Trust and Security page invites that scrutiny.

We couldStart with: X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, X-Frame-Options: DENY, Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin, Permissions-Policy: camera=(), microphone=(), geolocation=(), then a CSP that allows self, cdn.jsdelivr.net, fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com and the GoDaddy script (or drop that script, see below). Also hide the Server version with ServerTokens Prod. The portal deserves the same set plus Cache-Control: no-store on authenticated responses.

CriticalNo privacy policy, cookie policy or terms anywhere on the site

SeenNot one page mentions privacy, GDPR, DPDP, or terms. The footer has no legal links. The contact form collects full name, email and a mandatory mobile number with no notice of how the data is used. The cookie banner links "Cookie settings" and "Trust & Security" to pages/trust-security.html, which contains no cookie or privacy text at all.

Why it mattersRackTrack operates from India and the US and offers UK and German country codes on the form, so GDPR/UK GDPR and India's DPDP Act both apply to that form. A missing privacy notice is also one of the first things an enterprise security questionnaire asks about.

We couldPublish a Privacy Policy, Cookie Policy and Terms page, link them from the footer and from under the form's submit button ("By submitting you agree to our Privacy Policy"), and make the "Cookie settings" link go to a real settings panel or the Cookie Policy.

HighCookie consent is not honoured

SeenThe banner says "We use cookies for website analytics" and offers Decline. But the only tracker on the site, GoDaddy's tccl.min.js (injected by the host after </html> with the comment "Monitoring performance to make your website faster. If you want to opt-out, please contact web hosting support"), loads on every page before the banner appears and regardless of the choice. Declining only writes rt-cookie-choice=declined to localStorage and changes nothing else.

We couldEither turn the GoDaddy monitoring off in cPanel (it is not analytics you use) and simplify the banner, or gate any real analytics behind the Accept choice. A banner that offers a choice it does not act on is worse than no banner.

HighContact form has no backend, so leads can be lost

Seenjs/contact.js builds a mailto:info@racktrack.ai?subject=...&body=... link and sets window.location to it. Nothing is sent to a server. The success message says "Your email app is ready with the request details" even when no mail app opened.

Why it mattersOn a locked-down corporate laptop, a phone using Gmail in the browser, or any machine without a default mail client, the click does nothing visible. The visitor thinks they submitted; you never hear from them. There is also no record on your side of who asked, which makes the "Within 1 business day" promise impossible to track.

We couldPost the form to a small endpoint (a serverless function, Formspree-style service, or your portal backend), store the lead, send a confirmation email, and show a real "Thanks, we'll reply within one business day" state. Add a honeypot field or Turnstile for spam.

MediumThird-party script loaded without version pinning or integrity check

SeenGSAP and ScrollTrigger load from https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/gsap@3/dist/.... "gsap@3" is a floating version, and there is no integrity attribute.

We couldPin to an exact version (for example gsap@3.12.5) and add Subresource Integrity hashes, or self-host the two files next to your other JS (they are 73 KB and 42 KB).

MediumDeveloper notes shipped to visitors

Seenjs/about-us.js opens with a comment referring to an internal file: "same proven-safe pattern as trust-security.js ... see AGENTS.md for why". js/homepage.js and js/refresh-home.js carry long design-rationale comments. The stylesheet query strings read like commit messages (?v=why-racktrack-29-deck-copy-mobile-fix-sticky-glass-nav-no-deck-steps-hero-gap-inter-navsm2-btngap).

We couldMinify the JS and CSS for production (which strips comments) and use a short hash for cache busting.

LowSmall hygiene items

  • No /.well-known/security.txt, so researchers have no published way to report a vulnerability. Cheap to add and it looks good to security teams.
  • No CAA DNS record, so any certificate authority could issue a certificate for the domain. Add one naming your CA.
  • The plain-text email address on every page and in the form's mailto will be harvested for spam. Fine if you accept that; otherwise obfuscate or rely on the form.
  • TLS could not be inspected from this environment (outbound traffic is re-encrypted by a proxy here), so run SSL Labs once. Aim for an A and TLS 1.2 minimum.

This is the biggest practical problem on the site. The design relies on very large images and frame-by-frame animation, and nothing has been resized or compressed for the web. Measured transfer sizes:

CriticalImages are 4-9x larger than they need to be

SeenExamples: trust-security/hero-facility-aisle.jpeg 3.44 MB at 2816x1536, displayed at 1240 px wide; why-racktrack/why-step-scan-app.jpg 3.29 MB, displayed at 347 px wide; homepage/rack-exploded.jpeg 2.58 MB, displayed at 301 px wide. No image on the site uses srcset or sizes, so phones download the same files as a 4K monitor.

We couldExport every photo at 1x and 2x of its largest display size, as WebP or AVIF (a 1240 px WebP of that aisle photo would be ~150 KB, not 3.4 MB), and add srcset plus width/height attributes so the layout does not jump while loading. Expected result: most pages drop from 6-10 MB to under 1 MB.

HighThe 59 MB video

Seenassets/homepage/RACKTRACK.mp4 is 59.5 MB. preload="none" is set (good), but the moment someone clicks "Watch video" the browser starts pulling the whole file from a shared Apache host, so on a normal connection it stalls.

We couldRe-encode to H.264 at 1080p around 4-6 Mbps (typically 8-15 MB for a 2-minute clip), add a poster image, or host it on Vimeo/YouTube/Mux for adaptive streaming.

HighHero images set to lazy-load, so the first screen paints blank

SeenThe About Us hero image and the two figure images on Trust and Security use loading="lazy" even though they are at or near the top of the page. In testing, About Us showed an empty white box where the hero photo belongs for several seconds.

We couldRemove loading="lazy" from anything above the fold and add fetchpriority="high" to the one true hero image per page. Keep lazy loading for everything below.

HighCaching is working against you

Seenstyles.css (208 KB, 49 KB gzipped) is sent with cache-control: no-cache, must-revalidate, and every page references it with a different ?v= string (12 different ones across the site), so the browser downloads it again on almost every page. JavaScript files and all the animation .webp frames have no cache-control at all, so the browser guesses. HTML is max-age=0.

We couldUse one cache-busting hash for the stylesheet across all pages; serve CSS, JS, fonts and images with Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable (the font files already do this correctly); and keep HTML short-lived.

MediumFonts declared but not used

SeenThe stylesheet declares five Satoshi weights, Geist and Geist Mono, and Inter. The site actually renders in Inter and Geist Mono. Satoshi is only used inside the small "rack reader" widget on Solutions, and Geist (sans) is not used anywhere. The Knowledgebase loads yet another pair, IBM Plex Sans and Mono, from Google Fonts.

We couldDelete the unused @font-face blocks, switch the rack reader to Inter, and move the Knowledgebase onto the site's fonts. Fewer families also makes the site look more coherent.

MediumOne 208 KB stylesheet for every page

All 24 pages load the same 208 KB CSS file, which contains page-specific rules for every page. Splitting the shared base from per-page styles, or at least minifying (comments alone are a large share of it), would cut it by more than half.

Bugs and odd behaviour

CriticalPressing Refresh on any page sends the visitor to the homepage

Seenjs/refresh-home.js is loaded in the <head> of all 24 pages. If the navigation type is "reload", it calls location.replace(home). Its own comment says: "Pressing the browser's reload button on ANY page sends the visitor to index.html instead of re-rendering the page they were on."

Why it mattersRefresh is one of the most basic things a person does in a browser: after a flaky connection, after changing a setting, or to retry a form. Being thrown to the homepage from the middle of an article, a use-case page or the contact form reads as a broken site. It also breaks "reload to see the latest" for anyone you send a link to, and confuses analytics (every reload counts as a homepage view).

We couldDelete the script. The comment in homepage.js explains the original motivation (refreshing mid-way through the pinned homepage animation left people at a strange scroll offset with the header hidden). That is already solved in homepage.js by history.scrollRestoration = "manual" plus a scroll to top, and it only needed to apply to the homepage.

HighCookie banner covers the navigation and headings

SeenThe banner is position: fixed, 480 px wide, centred at the top of the viewport with z-index: 700. On desktop it sits directly over the nav links (Why RackTrack, Solutions, Use Cases, Resources and part of Trust & Security are hidden behind it). On a 390 px phone it takes the top 200 px, covering the header and the H1 on every page until dismissed. The Trust and Security H1 reads "En...le by architecture" behind it.

We couldAnchor it to the bottom of the viewport (bottom-left on desktop, full-width bottom on mobile), or make it a slim bottom bar. Keep it under the header's stacking context so navigation stays clickable.

HighContact form validation rejects real people

SeenName must match /^[A-Za-z][A-Za-z\s'.-]*$/, so "José Núñez", "Zoë", "Søren", "Müller" and any name in Devanagari, Telugu, Arabic or Chinese returns "Enter a valid name." Phone must match /^\d{10}$/, so a UK mobile typed as 07911 123456 (11 digits), a German number, or an Australian number in the form people actually write them fails with "Enter exactly 10 digits." Only five country codes are offered. Mobile number is mandatory even though email is also mandatory.

We couldAccept any non-empty name; accept 6-15 digits after stripping spaces, dashes and a leading zero; offer a full country list or a free-text field; make the phone optional. Every rejected submission is a lost conversation.

High"0% Truth fidelity" is the real text on About Us

SeenThe two big stats on About Us are written in the HTML as 0% and animated up to 99.2% and 34% by JavaScript when scrolled into view. Search engines, screen readers, reader modes, link previews and anyone with JavaScript blocked see "0% Truth fidelity" and "0% Manual operations reduced". In the full-page capture the counters had not fired either.

We couldPut the final numbers in the HTML and animate from them (or count up from 0 only after replacing the text). Better still, see the note under Copy about sourcing these figures.

MediumResources page hides five of nine articles

SeenThe library says "4 articles shown" and the filter offers All / Operations / Infrastructure / Compliance. Five published articles (DCIM vs Physical Reality, From Audit to Autonomy, Physical Layer, The Hidden Cost of Wrong Rack Inventory, the Topology Debt piece) are reachable only from "related articles" links, and they are tagged Strategy, Finance and Security, categories that do not exist in the filter. The featured card is also repeated word for word as the first item in the list below it.

We couldList all nine, align the categories, and drop the duplicate.

MediumVideo modal does not take keyboard focus

Clicking "Watch video" opens the dialog but focus stays on the button behind it. A keyboard or screen-reader user has to tab through the whole page to reach the player and the Close button. Escape does close it, which is good. Move focus to the Close button on open and return it to the trigger on close, and trap Tab inside the dialog while open.

MediumThe header disappears for most of the homepage

The sticky header is hidden while a section is pinned (the code comment confirms this), which is nearly all of the ~18,000 px desktop homepage. There is no way to reach Contact or Sign In from the middle of the story without scrolling to one end. A slim persistent header, or a floating "Get a demo" button, would keep the exit visible.

MediumSolutions page: large empty gap under the hero, and a carousel that keyboard users cannot reach

On a 1440 px screen there is roughly 150 px of blank space between "See how it works" and "Three steps. No mystery" (the hero has a fixed height). Further down, "Six surfaces. One verified record" shows three and a half cards with the fourth cut off; the strip is a horizontal scroll region that is not focusable, so keyboard users cannot see cards 4-6 (flagged by axe as scrollable-region-focusable). Two of the page's headings are also invisible without JavaScript (opacity 0 until revealed).

LowSmaller items

  • The Facebook footer link is a /share/18depF5xzA/ short link that redirects to a "people" URL with tracking parameters. Link to the page's canonical URL instead.
  • The Instagram link lands on a login wall for anyone not signed in to Instagram, which is Instagram's behaviour, but worth knowing before featuring it.
  • Both the desktop story section and its mobile stand-in are in the DOM at the same time with identical aria-labels ("RackTrack continuous rack story"); screen readers announce two landmarks with the same name. Add hidden to whichever is not in use, or give them different names.
  • The 404 page is the bare text "404 Not Found" in Apache's default style, with no navigation back to the site.
  • The favicon is assets/logo.jpg, a JPEG. There is no apple-touch-icon, no SVG icon and no web manifest, so bookmarks and home-screen icons look rough.

Accessibility

An automated axe scan was run on all 24 pages at desktop and mobile widths, then checked by hand. Foundations are good (see above). These are the gaps.

HighLow-contrast text on every page

SeenThe footer line "© 2026 RackTrack Inc. · Physical Infrastructure Intelligence for Data Centers · Patent Pending" is 11.5 px monospace at 65% opacity on a near-white ground, roughly 3:1 against the 4.5:1 minimum. Flagged on all 48 page views. Also below the line: the status chips on Trust and Security ("Verified" in light green, "In progress" in amber, 11 px), the section index labels on Trust and Security, the Knowledgebase left-rail labels, and the greyed related-article text on Resources before it animates in.

We couldRaise the footer text to 13 px at full opacity in the body colour, and darken the status chip colours (a green like #1f6b46 passes; #43b883 does not).

HighHidden checkboxes drive the menus and are exposed to assistive tech

SeenThe mobile menu and the two dropdowns are built on <input type="checkbox"> elements with no label text (axe: "Form elements must have labels", critical, on 23 pages). Keyboard focus lands on these invisible checkboxes when tabbing through the header, so a screen reader announces "checkbox, not checked" with no name. On the Knowledgebase the checkbox is aria-hidden="true" yet still focusable.

We couldUse a <button aria-expanded aria-controls> for the menu toggle and dropdowns (a few lines of JS), or at minimum add tabindex="-1" to the checkboxes and give the labels visible names.

HighKnowledgebase has no lang, no page description, and 21 images without alt text

Screen readers will guess the language; 21 of 37 screenshots have no alt attribute at all, so the only description is the caption where one exists. The page also has ids that start with a digit (#1-create-an-organization), which breaks CSS selectors and some in-page linking, and duplicated landmark roles without names.

MediumVery small text

Text below 12 px appears throughout: 7.5 px and 9 px labels in the Solutions "rack reader" and schematic ("Identified", "U42-38"), 9-10 px chips on the homepage cards ("RACK VIDEO", "CAPTURE"), 11 px uppercase eyebrow labels on every page, 11 px form labels on Contact, 11 px "← ALL ARTICLES" links. Uppercase monospace at 11 px is particularly hard to read. Consider 12 px as the floor and 13 px for anything a person needs to read rather than glance at.

MediumSmall click and tap targets

Desktop nav links are 17 px tall (the clickable area is just the text). On mobile: footer links 17-22 px tall, social icons 18x18 px, the logo link 24x24 px, phone and email links 17 px. The recommended minimum is 24x24 px (WCAG 2.2) and 44x44 px for touch. Add vertical padding to the links rather than changing the type size.

MediumDecorative alt on images that carry meaning

All 15 images on the homepage, and one or two on most other pages, use alt="". That is right for the animation frames and background posters, but the three "evidence" cards (Phone sweep, Rack facts, Evidence record) and the four story cards convey product steps and deserve a short description. The article and use-case pages do this well already.

LowOther accessibility notes

  • On the six use-case pages the logo link has no accessible name at mobile width (the "RackTrack" text is hidden and the anchor has no aria-label, unlike the homepage).
  • The Contact "Mobile Number" <label> wraps both the country-code <select> and the number input, so the label is ambiguous. Give each its own label.
  • The Why RackTrack before/after slider exposes a bare "↔" character to screen readers.
  • The Knowledgebase body text is fully justified, which creates uneven word gaps and is harder to read for people with dyslexia. Left-align it.

Typography, colour and UI

The system: Inter for everything, Geist Mono for labels, figures and the footer line; navy #0b1a2e-range ink, a saturated blue accent for buttons and links, cool off-white backgrounds. It is coherent and appropriate for the audience. Suggestions:

MediumThree type systems across one domain

Marketing pages use Inter + Geist Mono; the Solutions rack reader uses Satoshi; the Knowledgebase uses IBM Plex Sans + Mono with its own colours, nav, layout and no site header or footer. Clicking "Knowledgebase" in the main menu feels like leaving the site, and there is no link back. Bring the guide onto the site's header, footer and fonts (or at least add a "← racktrack.ai" link and the same colours).

MediumHeading punctuation and casing are inconsistent

Two-line headings end the first line with a full stop and the second without ("Captured facts. Ready to verify", "Six surfaces. One verified record"). About Us and Contact put full stops on every heading ("Why we built RackTrack.", "Engage the platform team."); the other pages don't. The same shared component reads "Read the theory. Then run it on your rack." on five articles and without the final stop on four. The homepage H1 is Title Case ("Turn Physical Racks Into Verified Infrastructure Data"); every other H1 is sentence case. Pick one rule (sentence case, no terminal full stop on headings) and apply it everywhere.

MediumMonospace is doing too much

Geist Mono is used for eyebrow labels, stat numbers, chips, figure captions, status badges, the footer legal line and the contact details. Used sparingly it signals "data"; used this widely, at 10-11.5 px uppercase, it becomes the hardest-to-read text on the page. Keep it for figures and the schematic; set labels, captions and the footer in Inter.

LowLayout notes

  • Solutions hero: ~150 px of empty space below the CTA on desktop (fixed hero height).
  • About Us "story" timeline: the three beats are staggered left / left / right with large empty areas between them; "The team" and "The build" sit far to the right with nothing on the left. A simple two-column timeline would read more calmly.
  • Use Cases: five of six role cards had empty image areas until the lazy images arrived; with 1200x800 JPEGs behind them, on a slow connection the grid is mostly blank boxes. Smaller images fix this.
  • The sticky "glass" header lets headings show through behind the nav text on scroll (visible on Resources: "Browse the RackTrack" behind the menu). A slightly more opaque header background would keep the nav crisp.
  • The homepage story text is set over bright photographic frames with a light scrim; mid-transition the headings blur and overlap ("Structured, not guessed" over the previous heading). Increasing the scrim or fading the outgoing text faster would help.
  • Buttons: "Get a demo" (pill), "See how it works →" (pill with arrow), "Request Platform Brief →", "Read the article →" all differ slightly in padding and arrow use. One primary and one secondary style would tidy this.

Mobile

CriticalKnowledgebase renders as a shrunken desktop page on phones

There is no <meta name="viewport">, so a phone lays the page out at 980 px and zooms out: three-column layout, unreadable 5-6 px text, pinch-to-zoom required for everything. This is the one page a customer opens on their phone in a data hall. Add the viewport tag and a single-column layout below ~900 px.

HighForm fields trigger auto-zoom on iPhone

Inputs and selects on Contact are 13.3-14 px. iOS Safari zooms the page in whenever a field under 16 px gets focus, and does not zoom back out. Set inputs to 16 px on touch devices.

HighCookie banner blocks the top of every page on phones

Covered above: the banner takes the top 200 px of an 844 px screen, hiding the header, menu button and the H1 until dismissed.

MediumSame multi-megabyte images on mobile

Why RackTrack 9.7 MB and About Us 9.5 MB on a phone. On a typical 4G connection in a data centre car park that is 10-20 seconds before the pictures appear.

MediumOpen mobile menu is translucent

The page heading shows through behind the menu items ("Engage the..." ghosting behind "Why RackTrack" on Contact). An opaque menu background would make it easier to read.

LowGood mobile decisions worth keeping

Switching the homepage from the frame animation to a static four-card grid under 980 px is the right call and saves ~27 MB. No horizontal overflow was found on any page at 390 px. The menu toggle is 40x40 px and body scroll locks while it is open.

SEO and web standards

HighDuplicate URLs with no canonical

https://racktrack.ai/, https://racktrack.ai/index.html, https://www.racktrack.ai/, http://racktrack.ai/ and http://www.racktrack.ai/ all serve the same page with status 200, and no page has a <link rel="canonical">. Search engines split ranking signals across all of them. Redirect www and HTTP to the bare HTTPS host, redirect /index.html to /, and add a canonical tag to every page.

HighNo sitemap, no robots.txt, no social preview tags, no structured data

/sitemap.xml and /robots.txt return 404. No page has Open Graph or Twitter Card tags, so links shared on LinkedIn (your main channel) show no image and a truncated title. There is no JSON-LD for Organization, Article or FAQ (the contact page has 12 FAQs that would qualify for rich results).

Add a sitemap listing the 23 real pages, a robots.txt pointing to it, og:title / og:description / og:image / og:url and twitter:card on every page, and Organization + Article schema.

MediumTitles and slugs

  • Four article titles are 92-101 characters and get cut off in search results, e.g. "The Hidden Cost of Unreconciled Infrastructure: A Framework for Quantifying Topology Debt | RackTrack". Aim for under 60.
  • Two article URLs do not match their titles: article-what-it-costs-when-nobody-checks-the-rack.html is titled "The Hidden Cost of Unreconciled Infrastructure..." and article-from-audits-to-always-on-records.html is titled "From Audit to Autonomy...". Either title is fine; make them agree.
  • The Knowledgebase has no meta description and the title is just "RackTrack Guide".
  • Articles have no publish date or author, which search engines and readers both use to judge freshness and credibility.

MediumHTML validity

A validator reports 749 issues across the pages. Most are cosmetic (self-closing void tags, lowercase doctype, single-quoted attributes in the injected GoDaddy script). Copy and content

All ~9,000 words on the marketing pages were read line by line. The writing is confident and mostly clean; there are no outright spelling errors. The issues are consistency, claims, and repetition.

CriticalStatistics with no source

  • Homepage: "40-60% truth decay", "3-6 weeks audit exposure", "20-40 min response latency" sit in a stat panel with no verb, no definition and no source. Resources and seven related-article blocks headline "Why every enterprise CMDB is 40% wrong" - and the article itself never states or supports the 40% figure; the number appears only in the title. Homepage says 40-60%, Resources says 40%.
  • About Us: "99.2% Truth fidelity" and "34% Manual operations reduced" with no method, sample or customer.

HighNine labels for one action

"Get a demo" (home), "See it in action" (Why), "Request platform brief" (Why, all six use cases), "Request a platform brief" (Solutions, Use Cases, Resources, all articles), "Request Platform Brief" (Contact, three times), "Request a Security Brief" (Trust), "Request a demo" and "See the platform" (About), "Contact RackTrack" (Trust), and the Contact H1 "Engage the platform team." One application shown as three cards overstates it. "The foundation of a multi-year technical moat" is investor language on a customer page.

How this was checked

A crawler followed every internal link from the homepage and found 24 unique pages (25 URLs including /index.html), 143 assets and 6 external links; all assets were HEAD-checked for status and size. HTTP headers, redirects, DNS (SPF, DMARC, MX, CAA), common exposed paths and the 404 behaviour were checked with curl. Every page was loaded in headless Chromium at 1440x900 and 390x844, with an axe-core accessibility scan, console and network error capture, computed font and size inspection, tap-target measurement, and full-page screenshots; key interactions (cookie banner, dropdowns, mobile menu, video modal, contact form validation, keyboard focus, no-JavaScript rendering) were tested by hand. HTML was run through html-validate. All visible text was extracted and read in full.

Two things could not be checked from this environment: the live TLS certificate and cipher configuration (outbound connections here are re-encrypted by a proxy), and real-network Lighthouse timings. SSL Labs and PageSpeed Insights will cover both in a couple of minutes each; the byte counts above are exact and are what will drive the PageSpeed score.

Suggested order of work. Week 1: HTTPS redirect and headers, remove refresh-home.js, move the cookie banner, fix the form validation, publish privacy/cookie/terms pages. Week 2: image pipeline (resize, WebP, srcset), video re-encode, caching rules, above-the-fold lazy-load fix. Week 3: Knowledgebase (viewport, lang, alt, intro, remove staff-only text, "####" headings), Resources listing, canonical/OG/sitemap. Then the copy pass: one CTA, one capture story, sourced numbers, heading style.
