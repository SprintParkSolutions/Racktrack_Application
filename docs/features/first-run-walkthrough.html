How a new organization is set up

This page shows the RackTrack setup as it really runs on the live servers, step by step, with screenshots from the phone app, the desktop browser and the portal. It explains what the setup is, who sees it, what each step asks and why, and what happens after the last step.

What the setup is

When the RackTrack owner creates an organization, they also create its admin account. The first time that admin signs in, on the phone app or on the portal, a card opens over the screen and asks for the facts RackTrack needs before the first rack photo: which buildings hold the racks, which halls are inside them, how many racks each hall holds, who approves changes, and the rules.

The card shows one step at a time. There are ten steps. Five of them must be completed; five can be skipped and filled in later. The only buttons are Back and Next, and Finish on the last step. Every field is saved the moment the admin leaves it, so the admin can stop half way and come back.

Who sees it and when

Only the organization admin sees the setup. It opens on its own until the five needed steps are done, then never again unless the admin opens it on purpose. The RackTrack owner is never sent into it.

Technicians never see it. The admin finishes the setup first and invites people afterwards, so a technician who signs in goes straight to Scan.

After Finish, everything lives under Organization settings: in the app under Profile, in the portal under Organization. Each section can be edited there on its own, and "Walk through again" opens the card again from step one.

* A red star after a labelThis field must be filled. Next does not work while a starred field on the step is empty; the empty fields are pointed out under the field and in one line at the top of the step.
No markThis field is optional. It can be left empty and filled in later under Organization settings.
"* Required" lineShown once under the step title, only on steps that have starred fields. Steps with no star at all (Systems, Vendors, Naming, Rules, Review) do not show it.

What happens behind the screen

  1. 01Each field is written to the server as soon as the admin leaves it. The grey "Saved" at the top of the card confirms the last write. Nothing is guessed on the admin's behalf: the time zone and short code are only proposed, and the country is always chosen by a person.
  2. 02The server keeps a setup state per organization. As long as a needed item is missing, the state says the setup is not done, and the card opens for the admin on the phone app and on the portal alike. The moment the last needed item is saved, the state changes and the card stops opening.
  3. 03Finish asks the server once more whether anything is still missing. If something is, the card names it. If not, the card closes: the app goes to Scan, the portal stays on the page that was open.
  4. 04A technician's sign-in never checks this state. They land on Scan whatever the state of the setup, because the admin sets up before inviting anyone.
  5. 05Passwords for switch access are stored encrypted and are never sent back to the screen. The screen only shows that a password is held.

Words used on the screens

Organization: the customer company. It has one admin and one or more datacentres.
Short code: 2 to 16 capital letters, digits or hyphens, used on labels, reports and the app sign-in.
Datacentre: one building that holds racks, with its own address, approver and people. Called a Site elsewhere in the portal.
Facility code: the code the building itself uses, for example in NetBox or a CMDB.
Space: a hall, room, floor, row or cage inside a datacentre.
Rack count: how many racks a space holds. Names are not needed here.
Approver: the person who accepts or rejects a change a scan wants to make to the record.
Primary contact: the person RackTrack writes to about the account.
System of record: where the official inventory lives, such as NetBox or ServiceNow.
Naming pattern: the shape of a label, for example RK-## for racks. # stands for a digit.
SNMP: a read-only way for RackTrack to ask a switch about its ports and serial number.
Rules: approve before write, never delete, where a ticket goes, how long photos are kept, rack height and counting direction.
