Is the field mapping right?

The NetBox research in this document is careful and accurate, and most of it should be kept exactly as it is. The problem is the starting point. The sheet was built from NetBox and ServiceNow inwards, so it covers half the objects RackTrack produces and cannot describe the things only RackTrack knows.

How this was checked

Every NetBox field name in the document was compared against netbox-4.6.10-openapi.json, the schema dump for the exact NetBox version our docker-compose pins. Every CMDB claim was compared against the tables our own integration reads and writes, in servicenow/cmdb_apply.py and server/lib/netbox/connectors/servicenow.js. RackTrack's own fields come from server/lib/netbox/model.js, which is the single place our object model is defined. Nothing below is an opinion about style. Each point is a difference between the document and one of those three sources.

What is right, and must not be redone

Keep this

The NetBox column is complete. For all 8 object types the document covers, it lists every single field that NetBox has, with the correct API name, in the correct place. I checked field by field and found nothing missing anywhere. Cable, IP Address, Subnet, VLAN and VRF are flawless, with nothing wrong and nothing left out.

Of the 261 NetBox field names given, 254 are exactly correct. That is the slow, unglamorous part of this job and it does not need doing again. Every problem listed below is about what surrounds that column, not about the column itself.

One claim I expected to be wrong and was not: rack_units as the CMDB field for rack height. Our own seed script uses exactly that field. Good catch.

The problems, explained

Sixteen points. Each one gives what the document says, what is actually true, and what to put in its place. Four are structural and matter more than the rest: numbers 12, 13, 14 and 16.

  1. It covers 8 object types. RackTrack produces 16.Missing
    The sheet saysThere are tables for Rack, Device, Port, Cable, IP Address, Subnet, VLAN and VRF.
    What is trueA single rack scan produces sixteen kinds of object, listed in order in model.js. Seven of the eight tables match one of them. The eighth, VRF, is something we never create. So nine object types have no table at all, and they are listed in points 2 to 4 below. The full list is in the reference table at the end of this review.
    Do thisAdd a table for each of the nine missing types before adding any more fields to the eight that exist.
  2. Patch panels are missing completely.Missing
    The sheet saysNothing. The words front port, rear port and patch panel do not appear anywhere in the document.
    What is trueNetBox models a patch panel as two separate object types, a front port and a rear port, paired together. That pairing is how a cable is threaded through a panel so that NetBox can compute the whole path from a switch, through the panel, to the far end. RackTrack builds both. It is also the one thing no amount of network scanning can ever discover, because there is nothing inside a patch panel to interrogate, which is precisely why a customer pays us to photograph it.
    Do thisAdd a Front Port table and a Rear Port table. NetBox has 24 fields on each. On the CMDB side both become rows in cmdb_ci_port. Do these two first, ahead of everything else on this list.
  3. Power ports and power outlets are missing.Missing
    The sheet saysNothing. The Rack table has a row for powerfeed_count and stops there.
    What is trueA power port is what a device draws power through. A power outlet is the socket on the PDU it draws from. RackTrack creates both. NetBox has 26 fields on a power port and 28 on a power outlet.
    Do thisAdd both tables. On the CMDB side the PDU is cmdb_ci_pdu, which is also missing from the document.
  4. Five things are written as columns when they are separate objects.Missing
    The sheet saysrack_manufacturer Manufacturer (via Rack Type) manufacturer rack_model Rack Type (rack_type) model_id device_manufacturer Manufacturer (via Device Type) manufacturer
    What is trueIn NetBox, Manufacturer, Device Type, Device Role, Site and Location are five separate objects, each with its own API endpoint and its own fields. They are not columns on a rack or a device. More than that, NetBox refuses to create anything out of order: you cannot create a device whose type does not exist yet, and you cannot create a rack without a site. Our exporter walks a strict sequence for exactly this reason. The phrase "via Rack Type" hides all of that. An engineer building from this sheet would not learn that these objects exist, would try to create a device first, and would get a rejection they could not explain.
    Do thisGive each of the five its own small table, and add a note that they must be created before racks and devices. On the CMDB side they collapse into plain fields on the CI row, which is a real difference worth showing rather than hiding.
  5. Seven NetBox fields do not exist.Wrong
    The sheet saysrack_cooling_capability Cooling Capability (cooling_capability) rack_cooling_capacity Cooling Capacity (cooling_capacity) device_cooling_method Cooling Method (cooling_method) device_cooling_intake_count Cooling Intake Count (cooling_intake_count) device_cooling_outflow_count Cooling Outflow Count (cooling_outflow_count) port_channels Channels (channels) port_channel_id Channel Id (channel_id)
    What is trueNone of these seven appear anywhere in NetBox 4.6.10. Not on the object listed, and not on any other object either. I searched the whole schema for each one. They read as if they were remembered from a different product, or from a NetBox version that does not exist.
    Do thisDelete all seven rows.
  6. Two model number rows point at a field that is not there.Wrong
    The sheet saysrack_model_number Model (via Rack Type) model_number device_model_number Model (via Device Type) model_number
    What is trueA NetBox rack type has model and nothing else resembling a model number, so there is nothing for the CMDB model_number to map onto. A device type does have a separate field for this, called part_number, which is almost certainly what was meant.
    Do thisChange the device row to part_number. On the rack row, write that NetBox has no equivalent, which is the honest answer.
  7. It says cables need a paid ServiceNow plugin. They do not.Wrong
    The sheet sayscable_a_end A-side Terminations A-end termination (TNI plugin only) cable_b_end B-side Terminations Z-end termination (TNI plugin only) cable_type Type (type) Cable / product model (TNI plugin only)Plus a note at the top: "base ServiceNow ITSM CMDB has no native Cable table at all".
    What is trueThe note is correct and the conclusion drawn from it is not. It is true there is no cable CI class. But a cable is a relationship between two ports, and ServiceNow models relationships in cmdb_rel_ci with a type of Connects to::Connected by. That is a base table, present in every instance, and our code already writes cables that way today. The document is inconsistent with itself as well: three rows further up it happily uses cmdb_rel_ci to express that a rack contains a device, then refuses to use the same mechanism for a cable.
    Do thisChange all three cable rows to cmdb_rel_ci with the Connects to type, and delete the plugin-only wording. This one matters commercially. As written, the sheet tells a customer we cannot deliver their cabling without buying a plugin, which is the opposite of true and gives away our best answer to the question they always ask.
  8. Four CMDB tables we write to are missing.Missing
    The sheet saysA port maps to cmdb_ci_network_adapter, and nothing else is named.
    What is trueOur integration writes to four more: cmdb_ci_port for patch panel ports, cmdb_ci_netgear for the panels themselves, cmdb_ci_server for servers, and cmdb_ci_pdu. cmdb_ci_network_adapter is right for a switch or server NIC and wrong for a panel port, and the sheet has only the one row for both.
    Do thisSplit the port row by what the port actually is, and add the four tables.
  9. Custom fields are sent to the wrong place.Wrong
    The sheet saysCustom Fields maps to attributes, on all four objects.
    What is trueIn ServiceNow a custom value lives in a u_ column added to the CI table. That is the convention, and it is the one we already follow: we write u_racktrack_scan_id onto the rack CI, and our reconcile step reads that exact field back to find the rack again on a rescan. If anyone built to attributes, the rescan would stop matching and every rack would be created twice.
    Do thisChange it to a u_ column, and name u_racktrack_scan_id as the worked example.
  10. Owner is mapped two different ways in the same document.Wrong
    The sheet saysIn the object tables, NetBox owner maps to managed_by_group. Separately, owned_by is listed as a CMDB-only field with no NetBox equivalent.
    What is trueBoth cannot be right at once. managed_by_group is who looks after it day to day, owned_by is who owns it. They are different questions and the sheet answers with each in turn.
    Do thisPick one, apply it to all four objects, and put a one-line note saying why the other was not chosen.
  11. VRF gets 19 researched rows for something we never make.Extra
    The sheet saysSection 5.4 has a complete and accurate 19-row VRF table.
    What is trueRackTrack does not produce VRFs. There is no VRF in our object model and no VRF in our export sequence. The table is well researched and describes work we do not do. Every CMDB cell in it is null, so it reads as a nineteen-line statement that neither side has anything, which is not the case: NetBox has all nineteen, we have none of them.
    Do thisMove it to a clearly marked future-scope section, or delete it. The same question applies to VLAN, where we hold 6 fields against NetBox's 22.
  12. The names are copies, not decisions.Structure
    The sheet saysThe first column is a unique name, described as the key that joins the two systems.
    What is trueI tested every row. In 431 of the 489 rows, 88 percent, the unique name is nothing but the object name plus whatever the other column already called the field. rack_serial is NetBox's serial. rack_model_number is the CMDB's model_number. port_poe_mode is NetBox's poe_mode. The column was generated, not decided, so it carries no information that is not already in the row.
    Do thisDecide each name from RackTrack's side, based on what we call the thing and what we would want to call it. Where NetBox's name is genuinely the best name, keep it on purpose rather than by default.
  13. It quietly proposes that RackTrack should hold about 400 more fields.Structure
    The sheet says107 rows under a rack, 119 under a device, 134 under a port.
    What is trueBecause every field on either side became a row, and every row got a RackTrack-shaped name, the sheet reads as a specification for what RackTrack should store. That means committing to a rack's outer depth and mounting depth, a device's virtual chassis priority, a port's PoE type and transmit power, and a CI's attestation status, cost centre, GL account and invoice number. RackTrack holds 10 fields on a rack today, 18 on a device, 10 on a port.
    Do thisThis is the decision to make consciously rather than inherit. If the intent is that RackTrack grows into the union of both systems, that is a large product commitment and should be written down as one. If not, the extra rows belong in a separate reference sheet, as in point 14.
  14. Only 65 of the 489 rows actually map across.Structure
    The sheet says489 rows of mapping across eight tables.
    What is trueA mapping row has to say "this thing here is that thing there". Only 65 rows have both sides filled in. The other 424 rows have one side null, which means they say "this exists in one system and not the other". That is genuinely useful research and it is not a mapping. It also skews every count in the document: the Port table looks like 134 mapped fields and is really 14. The document already half knows this, because the summary table at the end keeps exactly those 65 and drops the rest.
    Do thisSplit into two sheets. Sheet one is the mapping, rows where something maps. Sheet two is "fields we do not carry", the one-sided rows, kept because they answer a customer asking why a field is empty.
  15. Nothing that makes RackTrack distinctive is in it.Missing
    The sheet saysNothing. There is no row for any of them.
    What is trueThis follows directly from point 12. A name copied from NetBox or ServiceNow can only ever describe NetBox or ServiceNow, so anything we hold that neither of them holds could never have appeared. What is missing: the evidence level (both ends of LLDP agree, SNMP said so, read off a photo, a human typed it), the confidence score derived from it, the provenance naming the image or the OID that proved it, our stable id that makes a rescan update rather than duplicate, the port fingerprint that tells two identical unlabelled switches apart, and the conflict record for when two sources disagree. Some of these do have a home on the far side: the id goes in NetBox's racktrack_uid custom field and the CMDB's correlation_id, and the evidence level is what decides whether a NetBox cable is connected or planned. Others have no home anywhere, and saying so plainly is more useful than leaving them out.
    Do thisAdd a row for each, with "not supported" written honestly where that is the answer. Without them the sheet describes RackTrack as a thinner NetBox. With them it shows we know things NetBox cannot, and can prove where each one came from.
  16. One sheet is right. It needs a How column.Structure
    The sheet saysrack_contained_devices Device Count (device_count) Related Items (Contains::Contained by) device_rack_ref Rack (rack) Related Items (parent rack) port_device_ref Device (device) Device (reference)
    What is trueTo answer the question directly: keep one sheet. Two would mean maintaining the RackTrack column twice and they would disagree within a month. But one sheet only works if it records how each side stores the thing, not just what it is called, because the two systems are shaped differently rather than just named differently. "This device is in this rack" is a plain field in NetBox and a relationship row in ServiceNow. "This device is a Cisco" is a separate object in NetBox reached through the device type, and a field on the row in ServiceNow. A cable is an object with two ends in NetBox and has no object at all in ServiceNow. With only a field-name column there is nowhere to put that, so the sheet writes prose like "Related Items (Contains::Contained by)" into a column that is supposed to hold a field name, and nobody can build from it.
    Do thisUse these columns: RackTrack object, RackTrack field, NetBox object, NetBox field, How, CMDB table, CMDB field, How, Notes. Where each How is one of five values: direct field, reference to another object, relationship row, custom field, or not supported. That single column absorbs every piece of prose currently sitting in a field-name cell, and it is what lets one sheet serve both systems honestly.

Reference: the sixteen objects, and what RackTrack holds

This is the list the rebuilt sheet should be built from, in this order. The order is not cosmetic. NetBox rejects an out-of-order create, so a device cannot be written before its device type exists, and nothing can be written before its site. Field names are RackTrack's own, from model.js. Every object also carries uid, evidence and provenance, which are not repeated in each row.

What to change

In the order I would do them. The first two are the ones that change what the sheet is; the rest are corrections.

  • Rebuild starting from RackTrack's sixteen objects, one row per RackTrack field, instead of starting from NetBox's tables.points 1, 12
  • Add the How column for each side, with its five values, so references and relationships stop being written as prose.point 16
  • Add the nine missing object types. Front and rear ports first, then power, then the five that are currently hidden behind "via Rack Type".points 2, 3, 4
  • Delete the seven NetBox fields that do not exist.point 5
  • Change the device model number row to part_number, and mark the rack one as having no NetBox equivalent.point 6
  • Fix the three cable rows to cmdb_rel_ci Connects to, and remove the plugin-only claim.point 7
  • Add cmdb_ci_port, cmdb_ci_netgear, cmdb_ci_server and cmdb_ci_pdu, and split the port row by what the port is.point 8
  • Change custom fields from attributes to a u_ column.point 9
  • Settle owner on one of managed_by_group or owned_by, and use it everywhere.point 10
  • Add rows for evidence, confidence, provenance, the stable id, the port fingerprint and conflicts, with "not supported" where that is the truth.point 15
  • Move the 424 one-sided rows into a second sheet, "fields we do not carry".point 14
  • Move VRF to future scope, or remove it.point 11
