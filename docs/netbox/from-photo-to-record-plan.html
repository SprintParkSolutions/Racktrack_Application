From Photo to Record

One build order for the two identity questions, which rack and which device, taking the two standards we have already written and saying what to build, and in what order.

1 · Make identity real→ 2 · Safety net→ 3 · Good inputs→ 4 · Devices inside

The two documents say what the app should do. This one says in what order to build it, so each step stands on the one before and nothing risky ships without a net under it. Everything rests on one idea: identity comes from the hardware and the photo, never from a code we invented or a name someone typed.

1

Make identity real

the spine
  • Recognise the rack. Resolve a photographed rack to one the company already has, by its space, its name, the company's own naming pattern, and its label. When nothing is certain, ask a person rather than mint a new rack.
  • Give each device a hardware identity. A device becomes what the factory set, its chassis address or serial, not the rack someone typed it into. Every placement becomes a small record that carries where it came from, the date, and a confidence.

DeliversThe app knows which rack, and each device has an identity that survives a rename, a new address, or a move. This alone fixes the wrong-contact and saved-twice problems behind the work we just shipped.

2

A safety net

small, do it early
  • One end-to-end test. Run the whole path by itself, from photo to compare to save to checking it saved correctly, against a stand-in of the customer's database.

DeliversThe riskier phases after this cannot quietly break the save. Cheap to build, and it guards everything that follows.

3

Give the match good inputs

medium
  • Read the labels. Read the labels and barcodes people put on racks and gear, and use them label to record to wire: the record says what serial the name should have, and the live switch either shows it or it is stale. Never a nearest-name guess; a stale label loses to the wire and raises a low-priority re-label note.
  • Narrow before matching. Never compare against the whole estate. Start from what the records place in this rack, then what was here before, then neighbours, then what draws power here. If the shortlist stays too big to be honest, say "not sure" with the reason.

DeliversMatching starts from a short, honest list of candidates with strong signals, instead of the whole building and a guess from shape.

4

Sort out the devices inside

biggest
  • Place each device. Line each box up with the device the record expects at that shelf, by position first and then the stronger reads, a scanned code, a label, the print, the shape, the cabling pattern, a locator light, or a person. A moved device is told apart from one removed and one added.
  • Say how sure, and grade the gear. Every row comes out Confirmed, Probable, Possible, or Unidentified, and only the first two are written, the Probable ones marked as such. Each device is graded on what it publishes, so a rich device is not held back by a bare one beside it.

DeliversThe inside of the rack is trustworthy: every row carries a confidence, and a guess never reaches the customer's record.

Why this order. The spine comes first because nothing works until the app knows which rack and each device has an identity. The safety net comes second so the two large phases after it cannot break the save unseen. Good inputs come before the big matching, because the match is only as good as what feeds it. The devices inside are last, and largest, built on a base that is already tested.

The two questions in full: Knowing Which Rack and Knowing Which Device. Drawn from the Match and Reconcile plan and the Rack Binding Standard, draft 2. Nothing here is built yet; today the product satisfies one clause of the standard, the shelf-label repair.
