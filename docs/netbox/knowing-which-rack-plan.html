Knowing Which Rack

Four fixes so the app can tell which real rack a photo is, and trust what is inside it, before anything reaches the record.

15 September 2026from the Match and Reconcile plannothing built yet

The problem, in one breath

When a technician photographs a rack, the app makes up a code from the photo. It never links that photo to the real rack the company already has on record, so it cannot tell that this is the rack in the comms room, row 3. Two things go wrong: the app often cannot find the right person in charge of the rack, and it can save the same rack twice by mistake. These four fixes close that gap.

The four fixes

1

Recognise the rack

Foundation · start here

BuildTeach the app that a photo is a rack the company already knows, using the rack's location, its name, and its labels. When it cannot be sure, it asks a person rather than guessing.

WhyThis fixes the wrong-contact problem and the saved-twice problem, and everything below depends on the app knowing which rack it is looking at.

2

Read the labels

Smaller

BuildRead the labels and barcodes people stick on racks and gear, and use them to name things. A label is always checked against what the live network actually shows, because labels go out of date.

WhyA readable label or barcode is the fastest, most certain way to name a rack or a device.

3

Sort out the devices inside

Biggest

BuildLine each device in the photo up with the device the record expects at that shelf. Then a device that moved up a slot is told apart from one removed and one added. Only the sure ones are saved; anything unclear goes to a person to confirm.

WhyThis is what makes the inside of the rack trustworthy, not just the rack itself.

4

A safety test

Protects the rest

BuildA test that runs the whole thing by itself, from photo to compare to save to checking it saved correctly, so a future change cannot quietly break it.

WhyIt is cheap to build and it guards the other three from silent mistakes.

Where to start

1 · Recognise the rack→ 4 · Safety test→ 2 · Read the labels→ 3 · Devices inside

Recognising the rack comes first, because nothing else works until the app knows which rack it is looking at, and it also finishes the contact fix from the work we just shipped. The safety test comes next, so the riskier changes cannot break the save unnoticed. Then the labels, and last the devices inside, which is the largest piece.

Based on the Match and Reconcile plan. Nothing here is built yet; this document is for deciding what to build first, and in what order.
