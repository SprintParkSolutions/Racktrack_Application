RackTrack → NetBox: what we can and cannot do

We do not connect to NetBox today. What works is the harder half — a phone scan that reads the rack, plus live data from the switches. Getting it into NetBox is a translator we would build.

Why cables are the hard part

What we know about any cable comes from three places. They are not equally reliable.

1 · Seen

The port is occupied

The photo shows a cable in the port, its colour and its connector. Measured, with a confidence score.

2 · Guessed

Where the cable goes

We cannot see this — the cable vanishes into a bundle. So we assume cables hang straight down and pair each port with what sits below it.

3 · Confirmed

The switch names its neighbour

Ask a switch what is on the other end of its own cable and it answers. The only one of the three that proves anything.

Our rule for a trial: create cables in NetBox from the third kind only. The first two go in as suggestions for a person to check — never as silent writes. A tool that quietly invents cabling is worse than no tool, because the mistake outlives the visit.

Two limits worth knowing: we speak LLDP but not CDP, and Cisco uses CDP unless someone switches LLDP on — so on your kit we may ask and hear nothing. And patch panels and most servers cannot answer at all, which is exactly where the camera earns its place.

What we would need from you: one representative rack · a read-only login for its switches · whether LLDP is switched on for your Cisco kit · a test NetBox with an access token · one person who actually knows what is in that rack.

Internal — not for the customer

Backing for each claim, and two things to settle

  • No NetBox writer — NetBox exists only as a credential type and as a simulator serving /api/dcim/*. Real credential consumers resolve servicenow only.
  • Pairing is inferred — derive_wiring() matches switch-to-panel by smallest horizontal gap, falls back to ordered pairing; core uplinks are synthesised.
  • Demo data is flagged — synthetic: true sits on device records in generated topology.json, including model strings and IPs. Any import must filter it out.
  • LLDP real, CDP absent — per-vendor recipes run the LLDP commands and diff neighbours into drift events. No CDP parser exists anywhere in the tree.
  • Serials via SSH — captured from show version; OCR deliberately excludes long tokens as serials and MACs.
  • No asset tag, no webhooks — neither appears in any schema, export or route.

  • 2 · Name the CDP gap. A Slovak university is likely Cisco-heavy; hiding it would surface on day one of a pilot.
