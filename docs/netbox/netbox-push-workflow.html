NetBox Push Workflow

RackTrack · Workflow

Pushing a rack into NetBox

Three things happen. We compare, we work out what changed, and we hand it to one person who decides.

1

Compare

What NetBox thinks is in the rack, next to what we just saw.

We pull the rack's record out of NetBox and lay it beside the scan. Then we freeze the result, so it stops moving while people are looking at it.

Nothing is written. Nothing is decided. This is only the two pictures, side by side.

Why freeze it A live list changes every minute. If the admin approves something on Monday, we need to know exactly what they were looking at when they did.
2

Identify

Work out what actually changed, and how sure we are about each one.

The two pictures differ in a handful of places. We name each difference in plain words, and we say how confident we are:

  • Three switches moved shelf. We are sure — the hardware matches, only the position changed.
  • One was replaced. Same address, different hardware inside.
  • One is new. Nothing in NetBox matches it.
  • One we cannot place. Two switches in that rack look identical and nothing separates them.
Nothing is dropped here The one we cannot place still goes forward, just marked as uncertain. It is the admin's job to decide what to do about it, not ours to hide it.
3

Pass it to the admin

One person sees the whole list and decides each item.

The engineer who scanned the rack does not push it. The frozen list goes to the admin for that site, and they go through it item by item. Nothing is all-or-nothing.

Why one person However many people end up involved, there is still a single name against every change that reaches the customer's record.
Approve

It is clear enough to act on. The admin says yes.

Goes into the push
Raise a ticket

Somebody needs to go and look at the rack first. The admin picks who, and assigns it.

Comes back here when done
Reject

It is wrong, or it is not ours to change. The reason is kept.

Never written

When a ticket comes back, it lands on the same admin, carrying whatever the person found. They then approve it or reject it, exactly as above. A finished ticket does not write anything by itself.

Then, just before writing, we compare against NetBox one last time. If anything has moved since we froze the list, we stop and show the admin what changed.

And then it is written. Create what is missing, update what differs, delete nothing. Everything that happened is recorded — what changed, who approved it, who worked the ticket, when it went in.

The last check matters Approve at ten o'clock. Somebody edits NetBox at five past. Push at ten past. Without that check, their work is simply gone and nobody ever finds out.

Only the admin's approval writes

Not a scan, not a finished ticket, not a confident guess. Every change in the customer's record has one person's name on it.

Everyone talks about the same list

The approvals, the tickets, the push and the history all point at the one frozen list, so they can never tell different stories about the same rack.

A switch is its hardware, not its shelf

Track it by what the factory built into it. Track it by the slot, and a switch moving one shelf reads as one device gone and another appearing.

Who does what

EngineerScans the rack, checks the mapping, runs the comparison, hands it over. Cannot push.
AdminSees everything. Approves, tickets or rejects each item, picks who works a ticket, and pushes — for their own site.
Whoever gets the ticketGoes and looks at the rack, sorts it out, sends the answer back to the admin. Cannot push.
NobodyDeletes anything from NetBox. There is no way to, on purpose.

This is how it should work, not how it works today. Right now the comparison works and the push works, and nothing joins them — nothing forces a comparison first, nothing re-checks NetBox before writing, and the list of differences is shown on screen and then thrown away. There is no admin step, no ticket, and no history. And the only person who can push at all today is us, not the customer.
