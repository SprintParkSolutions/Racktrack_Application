RackTrack · planning note · 17 September 2026

The drift approval specification, explained, and a plan to build it

Your manager sent a 17-page functional specification for a full drift ticketing and approval platform. This page says in plain words what it asks for, what RackTrack already does, what is missing, and how it could be built in stages. Phase 0.5, the fixes to our own frozen rules, was built on 17 September; everything else waits for the decisions in section 08.

Read this first. The specification keeps the rules we froze on 11 September: the technician never decides, the admin assigns before deciding, a resolved ServiceNow ticket is not an approval, only an approval writes to NetBox, and nothing is written if NetBox moved. What it adds is everything around those rules: a verification re-scan before approval, a full status model, SLA clocks, escalation, notifications, reports, exceptions, an audit trail, and server-side permissions for every action.

01

What the specification asks for, in plain words

The document describes a product, not a feature. Its main ideas:

  • Three separate records instead of one. A drift is a difference between what NetBox says and what the technician saw. A work ticket is the job of fixing it, usually in ServiceNow. A change approval is the permission to write the corrected state into NetBox. Keeping them apart stops a fixed ticket being mistaken for permission to write.
  • A longer path. Scan, drift found, technician submits, admin triages, admin assigns the contact, ServiceNow ticket, contact investigates and resolves, technician re-scans to verify the fix, admin approves, system writes to NetBox, post-write check, complete. The two verification steps are new for us.
  • Eight roles. Technician, SPOC or resolver, admin or triage, change approver, auditor, management, super admin, system integration. Each with what they may and may not do, enforced on the server, not by hiding buttons.
  • Thirteen screens. Dashboard, My queue, scan detail, drift list, drift detail, create drift, admin triage, SPOC workbench, verification, approval, write execution, reports, administration.
  • A status model with 17 states from Draft to Completed, with rules on who may move a record from one state to the next, every move recorded with actor, time, old state, new state and reason. Status and disposition are separate: a record can be Completed and Accepted Drift, or Completed and False Positive.
  • Fixed lists for dispositions, reject reasons, pending reasons, reopen reasons, and exception fields.
  • SLA clocks per priority (P1 to P4) for acceptance, investigation, resolution and approval, with business calendars, pauses, warnings at 80 percent, breach at 100 percent, escalation at 120 percent.
  • Integration rules. ServiceNow tickets must be created once (no duplicates on retry), synchronised back, and never approve anything. NetBox writes must re-read the object first, write only the exact approved payload, keep before and after snapshots, and never mark a failed write as complete.
  • Notifications for thirteen events over in-app, email and Teams, with delivery history and no duplicates.
  • Data model of nineteen tables with foreign keys, append-only audit events and soft delete.
  • Reports and dashboards where every number can be clicked to see the records behind it.
  • Twenty acceptance criteria and a delivery plan of five phases in eight sprints.
02

What RackTrack does today

Checked in the code on 17 September, not from memory.

  • The five-step workflow exists and is frozen in docs/design/drift-approval-workflow.md: check, assign, resolve, decide, write.
  • App, Drift check. The technician runs the compare from the report page, sees each difference as "NetBox says X, you saw Y", sees who the check will go to (the rack's contact from NetBox, or "the admin will choose"), can rename the rack and add a note, and sends it to the admin. They cannot approve, assign or write.
  • Portal, Approvals. The admin sees each submitted plan, assigns each item to the NetBox contact or another person, which raises a ServiceNow incident and emails the person. Approve and Reject appear only after the ticket is resolved. "Write the approved changes to NetBox" writes only approved items.
  • Portal, Incidents, drift tab. Every drift ticket with assignee, state and finding, read-only.
  • Portal, My checks. The technician's view of what became of each item.
  • ServiceNow. Incident creation is already idempotent: a correlation id per rack and item means a retry comments on the open incident or reopens a closed one instead of creating a second. State is read back from ServiceNow, but only when the admin opens the plan.
  • NetBox write. Before writing, the plan is compared again against live NetBox; if anything moved, nothing is written and a fresh plan comes back. Nothing is ever deleted. A round-trip test proves a second write changes nothing.
  • Isolation. Every plan belongs to one organization; no owner bypass.
  • Tests. Unit tests on plans, tickets, rack matching and the round trip.

04

What the code check found that should be fixed first

These are places where the code did not match the rules we already froze. All eight were fixed and deployed on 17 September 2026 as Phase 0.5 (ticket SPRTMS-1684; walkthrough in the ticket). They are kept here so the list stays honest about where we started.

  • Approve without assign is possible. The server accepts an approve or reject on an item that was never assigned. The portal hides the buttons until the ticket is resolved; the phone app does not. Rule 2 says assign first. Fix: refuse it on the server.
  • A failed write looks complete. If some objects fail during the NetBox write, the plan is still marked as applied and nobody is told. Fix: a "write failed" state and an alert.
  • No audit entries. Submit, assign, resolve, decide and write never reach the audit table. Fix: one audit row per transition.
  • Technicians cannot run the check. A member is refused by the NetBox routes; only site managers and above can compare. Fix: allow members to compare and submit, nothing more.
  • Two dead controls in the portal. "Assign the whole rack" and "ports follow this device" are shown but not implemented on the server. Fix: implement or remove.
  • My checks shows everyone's plans, not only the technician's own. Fix: filter by the caller.
  • The assignee is stored as a name, not as a contact id. Two people with the same name would collide. Fix: store the NetBox contact id and the email.
  • The drift ticket list is not admin-only on the server, only in the portal navigation. Fix: gate it.

06

The plan

The specification's own five phases fit us well. I have adjusted them to what exists, and added a short phase zero and a half to fix the mismatches above before building on them. Weeks are my estimate for one developer working with me; they need to be confirmed once the decisions in section 08 are made.

  1. Phase 0

    Decisions and design

    1 week · no code

    Answer the questions in section 08. Agree the words: drift, work ticket, change approval, disposition. Agree the role mapping onto our four roles or add new ones. Write the status machine and the API contracts as one document. Design the database tables that replace the plan JSON files, and the migration for the plans that exist.

    Exit: the design is approved and the ServiceNow and NetBox test instances are available.

  2. Phase 0.5

    Make today's code match the frozen rules

    done, 17 September 2026, ticket SPRTMS-1684

    • Refuse approve or reject on the server for an item that was never assigned; take the early buttons off the phone app.
    • Write an audit row for submit, assign, ticket raised, resolve, decide and write.
    • A "write failed" outcome with an alert to the admin; a plan with failures is not "applied".
    • Let a member run the compare and submit; keep everything else above them.
    • Store the assignee as a contact id and email; filter My checks to the caller; gate the ticket list; implement or remove the two dead portal controls.
  3. Phase 1

    Core workflow to the specification

    3 weeks

    • Move plans from JSON files into database tables: audits, drifts, drift items, work tickets, approvals, comments, audit events.
    • The status model: Draft, Submitted, Triage, Assigned, Accepted, In progress, Pending, Resolved, Verification pending, Approval pending, Approved, Rejected, Write in progress, Written, Write failed, Completed, Reopened. Every transition guarded on the server and recorded.
    • Fields: category, severity, priority, risk, disposition, reason codes from fixed lists.
    • Roles: technician, SPOC, admin, approver, auditor; each route checks them. Queues per role: My queue, admin triage, SPOC workbench.
    • Comments as a thread with visibility.

    Exit: submit, triage, assign, resolve works end to end with a full history.

  4. Phase 2

    Verification and approval

    3 weeks

    • Verification step: the technician re-scans the rack after the fix, the system compares the new scan with the proposed state, pass or fail, evidence attached, fail reopens.
    • Approval record bound to the payload hash and the plan version; a changed payload invalidates it. Second approver for high risk if the manager wants it.
    • Before and after NetBox snapshots, the API result per object, retry for a failed write, manual review state.
    • Post-write verification: compare again after the write and only then mark complete.

    Exit: no write is possible without a valid approval; verification gates pass.

  5. Phase 3

    SLA, notifications and reports

    3 weeks

    • SLA clocks for acceptance, investigation, resolution and approval; priorities P1 to P4 with the defaults from the specification; business calendars and site time zones (we already store the time zone per datacentre); pause on pending states; warn at 80, breach at 100, escalate at 120.
    • Notifications for the thirteen events over in-app, email and Teams, with templates, preferences, delivery history and de-duplication. The email path exists; Teams needs the owner's tokens.
    • Dashboard and reports with clickable numbers; CSV export.

    Exit: an operations team can run the backlog and see breaches.

  6. Phase 4

    Exceptions and scale

    2 weeks

    • Accepted drift and known exceptions with owner, scope, expiry and review date.
    • Duplicate detection when the same rack is compared again; parent and child drifts (a device and the ports that follow it); bulk assign; maintenance windows; reopen with a counter.
  7. Phase 5

    Intelligence, later

    not estimated

    Duplicate suggestions, SPOC recommendation, summaries, recurring drift insight, predicted SLA warnings. Advisory only; the rules do not change.

How it would go into Jira

One epic per phase. Inside each, tasks with two or three sub-tasks, four or five only for the heavy ones, following the rule we already use. No tasks for documents. Each task gets its report attached when it is done, as now.

07

What stays exactly as it is

  • The six frozen rules. The specification restates them; nothing in the plan weakens them.
  • ServiceNow stays the work system. RackTrack stays the system of record for evidence, verification and the write permission.
  • Nothing is ever deleted in NetBox, and nothing is written if NetBox moved.
  • The technician's screen stays simple. Compare, see who it goes to, send. The verification re-scan is the one addition on their side.
08

Decisions your manager needs to make before Phase 1

The specification lists ten of these itself. I have put them in plain words and added four from the code check.

1. ServiceNow record typeWhich drift categories create an Incident, a Task or a Change Request? Today everything is an Incident.
2. What RackTrack may write in NetBoxWhich objects and fields. Today: racks, devices, interfaces and their supporting records, never a delete.
3. Second approverIs there a risk level that needs two approvals? Who is the backup approver?
4. SLA pausesWhich pending states stop which clock.
5. Business calendarsWorking hours and holidays per site. We already hold the time zone per datacentre.
6. Evidence rulesWhich evidence is mandatory for which drift category: photo, re-scan, ServiceNow note.
7. RetentionHow long photos, evidence and audit events are kept. The setup already asks for photo retention days.
8. Notification channelsWhich are mandatory: email, Teams, in-app, SMS or push. Teams needs the tokens still owed to me.
9. IdentitySingle sign-on provider and the source of truth for users. Today: RackTrack accounts plus social sign-in.
10. VolumeExpected number of audits and drifts per day, and how many people at once.
11. Roles (ours)Map the eight roles onto our four, or add approver, auditor and management as new roles. My suggestion: add them as roles, keep owner as super admin.
12. Phone approvals (ours)May an admin approve from the phone app at all, or only from the portal? The specification puts approval at a desk.
13. Storage move (ours)Agree to move plans from JSON files into database tables in Phase 1, with a one-time migration of existing plans.
14. Verification by whom (ours)Must the re-scan be done by a different technician than the one who reported the drift?
09

Risks

  • Size. This is three to four months of work. The specification is written as a product; the phases let it ship in usable slices, starting with the fixes in Phase 0.5.
  • ServiceNow sync. Today we only read ServiceNow state when the admin opens a plan. A timer or a webhook is needed for SLA clocks to be truthful; the customer's ServiceNow admins must allow one.
  • Teams notifications depend on tokens we do not have yet.
  • Verification re-scan depends on rack identity being reliable, which is the next rung of the recognition work already planned.
  • Defaults for SLA times, priorities and escalation in the specification are marked as recommendations; they must be confirmed with operations before they are switched on.

Phase 0.5 is done and live. Everything from Phase 1 on waits for your manager's answers to section 08.
