Drift to Record

One difference found at a rack, carried to the record — from the technician’s phone, through the admin’s decision and a real ServiceNow incident, and back. Six steps, each a real screenshot from the running system.

1

Compare the rack, send it to the admin

Technician · phone

In the field, the technician scans the rack and the app compares it to NetBox — here, 57 things do not match. They add a note and tap Send to the admin. There is no write button: the person at the rack never changes the record.

2

It reaches the admin

Admin · portal

It lands in the admin’s Approvals inbox in the portal — who sent it, when, and how many changes there are to decide.

3

Assign it to the SPOC

Admin · portal

The admin does not judge the rack from a desk — they hand each difference to a person. NetBox names the rack’s single point of contact as the default; when NetBox names none, the admin picks someone. Approve and reject appear only after it comes back.

4

A real ServiceNow incident

System · ServiceNow

Each assignment raises a real incident on the developer instance, keyed to the rack and the thing so a monthly rescan reuses it instead of duplicating. Six of them, INC0010001 to INC0010006.

5

They check it, and resolve it

Assignee · ServiceNow

The assignee goes to the rack and resolves the incident with a finding. INC0010004 is Resolved; the activity log shows RackTrack flipped it from In Progress with the note “Checked the rack — device is present and correct.”

6

The technician sees it has gone

Technician · phone

Back on the phone, the technician sees it is with the admin — nothing written — and who anything unclear routes to. Each item shows where it stands. A resolved incident returns to the admin to decide; only the admin’s approval writes.

Every image is the live system on 11 September 2026: the phone app and portal at racktrack.ai, and the ServiceNow developer instance dev322173, whose incidents RackTrack raised and resolved. The rule this shows is frozen at drift-approval-workflow.md: the technician never decides, the admin assigns before deciding, a resolved ticket is not an approval, and only the admin’s approval writes.
