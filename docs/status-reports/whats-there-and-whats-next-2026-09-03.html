Why the phone can't reach the switch yet

The phone is on the right network and it can reach the switch. The problem is that it isn't the one doing the connecting.

Today · tapping "read this switch"
Phoneon the office Wi-Fi → Our serverrented machine, on the internet ✕ The switch10.10.1.33, inside the office

The phone asks the server to do the job. The server sits outside the customer's building and has no route to a private address inside it. It's like standing next to your neighbour's door and phoning someone abroad to knock on it.

What step 3 changes
Phoneon the office Wi-Fi → The switch10.10.1.33, same network → Our serverstores what came back

The phone talks to the switch directly, then sends the result up to be saved. Nothing needs opening on the customer's firewall, and the credentials never have to travel anywhere.

Why isn't it already built this way? The screens in RackTrack are web pages, and a web page isn't allowed to open the kind of direct network connection SNMP needs. The installed app can — but only with a small piece of native code added to it, which means building and shipping a new version to testers. That's the whole of step 3, and it's the one thing on this list nobody has proven yet.

If it works, and if it doesn't

If it works — the product does exactly what you've been describing: someone on their own Wi-Fi, standing at a rack, reading their switches through RackTrack. It also fixes something that has never worked, rather than adding something new.

If it doesn't — the part that reads switches has to run on a machine inside the customer's network instead. Still workable, but a different shape of product and a different conversation with the customer.

One app build answers it. Steps 1 and 2 don't depend on the answer and can start now.
